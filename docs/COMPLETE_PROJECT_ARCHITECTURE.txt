REQRIPPLE
SOFTWARE REQUIREMENT CHANGE IMPACT ANALYSER
Complete Project Architecture and Implementation Blueprint

Document purpose
This document defines the product vision, problem statement, scope, architecture, workflows, data model, impact-analysis logic, interfaces, implementation plan, testing strategy and development-agent guidance for the SEPM project.

Project level: Student project, medium-to-high difficulty
Recommended implementation style: Modular monolithic full-stack web application
Primary goal: A working, explainable requirements change-management product — not a static dashboard or an AI-chatbot wrapper.

===============================================================================
1. PROJECT IDENTITY AND VISION
===============================================================================

Product name: ReqRipple
Full title: Software Requirement Change Impact Analyser
Tagline: Understand the consequences of every software requirement change.

Product vision:
ReqRipple helps software teams understand what may be affected when a requirement changes. It maintains traceability between requirements and downstream engineering artifacts, analyses change propagation, explains why artifacts are affected, prioritises review work, and tracks whether the resulting updates and tests have been completed.

The word "ripple" represents the chain reaction caused by a seemingly small requirement change. A change to one requirement may affect acceptance criteria, design elements, software modules, API contracts, database entities, user-interface flows, test cases and documentation.

Product category:
Requirements engineering, requirements traceability, change management and software project quality assurance.

Primary users:
- Project Manager: oversees the project and approves or defers changes.
- Business Analyst / Requirements Analyst: creates requirements, evaluates change requests and maintains traceability.
- Developer / Technical Lead: reviews affected implementation artifacts and carries out updates.
- Tester / QA Engineer: reviews affected test cases and verifies changed behaviour.
- Stakeholder / Client Representative: submits requests and reviews decisions where permitted.
- Viewer: reads project status and reports without editing project data.

===============================================================================
2. PROBLEM STATEMENT
===============================================================================

In software development, requirements frequently evolve because of changing customer expectations, business decisions, regulatory conditions, feedback and technical constraints. Requirements are connected to many other parts of a software product, including user stories, acceptance criteria, design components, modules, APIs, database structures, user-interface screens, test cases and documentation.

When a requirement changes, teams often struggle to identify every directly and indirectly affected artifact. The relationships may be scattered across spreadsheets, documents, issue trackers, diagrams, messages and individual team members' knowledge. A small change may therefore be implemented in one place but missed in another.

This can result in inconsistent implementations, incomplete testing, outdated documentation, rework, defects, unexpected cost and schedule changes, and poor accountability for change decisions.

ReqRipple addresses this problem through a centralized, traceability-driven change-impact workflow. It stores project requirements and related artifacts, maps their dependencies, compares proposed and approved requirement versions, traverses the dependency graph, identifies affected items, explains the propagation paths, prioritises the items for human review and tracks their resolution.

Important boundary:
ReqRipple is a decision-support system. It does not claim to understand an entire source-code repository automatically or guarantee that every possible impact has been discovered. The quality of its analysis depends on the completeness and correctness of the traceability relationships entered and maintained by the project team. The product must make missing or uncertain traceability visible rather than hiding it.

3. PRODUCT OBJECTIVES
===============================================================================

O1. Maintain a structured, versioned repository of software requirements.
O2. Maintain a traceability graph connecting requirements to engineering artifacts.
O3. Record change requests with their reason, requester, urgency and proposed change.
O4. Compare the existing requirement version with the proposed version.
O5. Identify direct and indirect impacted artifacts through graph traversal.
O6. Explain every impact using readable dependency paths.
O7. Prioritise impacted artifacts using a transparent, configurable scoring model.
O8. Allow reviewers to approve, reject or defer proposed changes.
O9. Track decisions and remediation work for affected artifacts.
O10. Identify affected artifacts that have not been reviewed, updated or retested.
O11. Provide dashboards and reports for traceability health, change history and unresolved change work.
O12. Maintain an audit trail of important project actions.

Success is measured by working end-to-end workflows, correctness of graph traversal, explainability of impact results, reliable version history, usable reports and meaningful automated tests — not by the number of screens.

4. WHAT MAKES REQRIPPLE DISTINCTIVE
===============================================================================

The project must go beyond a basic requirements list or a dependency graph. Its distinctive value comes from combining the following capabilities.

4.1 Explainable impact paths
For every affected artifact, show the path from the changed requirement to that artifact.
Example:
REQ-03 Event Registration
  -> MOD-02 Registration Service [implemented_by]
  -> API-04 Register for Event [exposes]
  -> UI-07 Event Registration Screen [consumed_by]
  -> TEST-12 Registration API Integration Test [verified_by]

The user can inspect why an artifact was included rather than receiving an unexplained impact label.

4.2 Change Simulation / What-if Preview
Before approval, the user can analyse a proposed change without modifying the approved requirement baseline. The system creates a preview containing affected artifacts, impact priorities, affected owners, missing test coverage and estimated remediation effort. The preview is a simulation; it must not create permanent approved versions or remediation tasks until the change is approved.

4.3 Change Debt Dashboard
Change debt represents unresolved work created by approved changes. For example, if an approved change affects a high-criticality API and nobody has reviewed or updated it, that unresolved work remains visible. The dashboard highlights unacknowledged artifacts, overdue remediation, pending retests and accepted risks.

4.4 Traceability Health Monitor
The product identifies requirements with no implementation links, implemented artifacts with no verification links, orphan artifacts, broken links, requirements with incomplete coverage and critical components that have no mapped tests.

4.5 Evidence-backed decisions
A reviewer can see the changed fields, the dependency path, the reason for each impact score, the assigned owner, the disposition and the evidence supplied when marking an artifact as unaffected or updated.

4.6 Requirement volatility history
Show which requirements have changed repeatedly, how often they change, and which project areas receive repeated change requests. This helps teams recognise unstable requirements and areas at risk of rework.

4.7 Human-controlled analysis
The engine recommends impacted artifacts and priorities. Humans make final decisions. Users can mark a suggested impact as not applicable, but must provide a reason. The application must never silently remove an impact because a model or heuristic is uncertain.

4.8 No mandatory paid AI service
The core project works with deterministic rules and graph algorithms. Optional AI-assisted wording suggestions may be added later, but the project must remain fully functional without an API key or paid language model.

6. CORE DOMAIN CONCEPTS
===============================================================================

6.1 Requirement
A statement of a need or capability that the software must satisfy. Each requirement has a stable identifier, title, description, type, priority, criticality, status, owner and version history.

Suggested requirement types:
- Functional
- Non-functional
- Business
- Security
- Performance
- Usability
- Regulatory / Compliance

6.2 Requirement version
An immutable snapshot of a requirement at a point in time. It stores the version number and the contents that were approved. Do not overwrite an approved version. A new approved change creates a new version.

6.3 Engineering artifact
Any project item that may be affected by a requirement change.

Supported artifact types:
- User Story
- Acceptance Criterion
- Design Component
- Software Module / Service
- API Endpoint / Contract
- Database Entity / Schema
- UI Screen / User Flow
- Test Case
- Documentation Item
- Deployment / Configuration Item
- Project Task (optional in core release)

Every artifact should have: ID, title, type, description, criticality, owner, status, estimated effort, last-reviewed date and optional external reference.

6.4 Traceability edge
A directed relationship between two nodes in the project traceability graph. The edge records the relationship type, whether impact propagation is enabled, the relationship strength, its confirmation status and optional notes.

Important direction convention:
The edge is directed in the direction that impact is expected to propagate. If changing source node A may require review of target node B, store A -> B.

Examples:
- Requirement -> Module (implemented_by)
- Requirement -> Acceptance Criterion (specified_by)
- Requirement -> Test Case (verified_by)
- Module -> API (exposes)
- API -> UI Screen (consumed_by)
- Database Entity -> API (used_by)
- Component -> Documentation (documented_by)

A graph may contain cycles. The analysis engine must detect/avoid infinite traversal.

6.5 Change request
A proposed modification to an existing requirement. It stores the requester, reason, urgency, proposed content, affected requirement, status, submission date and decision history.

6.6 Impact analysis
A saved, reproducible snapshot of the graph and requirement version used for an analysis. It stores the analysis timestamp, algorithm version, result summary and individual impact results.

6.7 Impact result
A result for one potentially affected artifact. It stores shortest path distance, selected explanation path(s), score components, final score, priority band, evidence and review status.

6.8 Remediation action
A task created after a change is approved. It records what an owner must inspect, update, document or retest, along with due date and completion evidence.

6.9 Baseline
A named snapshot of the approved requirements and traceability state at a particular milestone. Baselines make it possible to compare project states over time.

8. END-TO-END BUSINESS WORKFLOW
===============================================================================

WORKFLOW A — PROJECT SETUP
1. User creates a project and enters its name, description and domain.
2. User adds team members and assigns roles.
3. User creates requirements with unique IDs.
4. User creates engineering artifacts.
5. User establishes directed traceability relationships.
6. System calculates initial traceability-health indicators.
7. User optionally saves a baseline.

WORKFLOW B — CHANGE REQUEST SUBMISSION
1. A stakeholder or analyst selects an existing requirement.
2. User enters the proposed title/description/acceptance criteria or other changeable fields.
3. User records the reason for change, business justification, urgency and requested date if applicable.
4. System compares the current approved version with the proposed content.
5. System stores the proposal as a change request. The approved requirement remains unchanged.
6. Change request moves to SUBMITTED.

WORKFLOW C — IMPACT ANALYSIS
1. Analyst opens a submitted change request.
2. System loads the approved requirement version and proposed version.
3. Diff engine identifies changed fields and produces a readable comparison.
4. Impact engine starts traversal from the changed requirement node.
5. Engine follows only active, propagation-enabled traceability edges.
6. Engine records affected nodes, shortest path distances and explanation paths.
7. Engine checks artifact criticality, relationship strength and verification coverage.
8. Engine calculates an impact-priority score and priority band.
9. Engine identifies missing or weak traceability as an analysis limitation.
10. System stores the analysis snapshot and displays the report.

WORKFLOW D — REVIEW AND DECISION
1. Approver examines the proposed change and its impact report.
2. Approver can inspect each affected artifact and the path explaining its inclusion.
3. Reviewer may add comments, request more analysis, approve, reject or defer.
4. If rejecting or deferring, rationale is mandatory.
5. If approving, the system creates a new immutable approved requirement version.
6. The old approved version remains accessible in version history.
7. System creates remediation actions for affected artifacts requiring updates or retests.

WORKFLOW E — REMEDIATION AND VERIFICATION
1. Each impacted artifact is assigned a disposition:
   - UPDATE_REQUIRED
   - RETEST_REQUIRED
   - REVIEW_NO_CHANGE
   - NOT_APPLICABLE
   - RISK_ACCEPTED
2. NOT_APPLICABLE and RISK_ACCEPTED require a written rationale and reviewer identity.
3. Owners update the artifact or record why no update is necessary.
4. Tester records test outcome: NOT_RUN, PASSED, FAILED or BLOCKED.
5. Failed or blocked tests remain visible and prevent normal closure where they are mandatory.
6. System recalculates unresolved change debt.
7. Change request can close only when closure conditions are satisfied or an authorised residual-risk decision is recorded.

WORKFLOW F — REPORTING
1. User opens project dashboard.
2. Dashboard presents open change requests, high-priority impacts, overdue actions, test gaps and traceability-health indicators.
3. User filters by requirement, module, owner, priority, status or date.
4. User exports an impact report or change history.

13. TRACEABILITY GRAPH DESIGN
===============================================================================

The graph is the core of ReqRipple.

Node categories:
- Requirement nodes
- Acceptance-criterion nodes
- Design nodes
- Module/service nodes
- API nodes
- Database/schema nodes
- UI nodes
- Test nodes
- Documentation nodes
- Deployment/configuration nodes
- Optional project-task nodes

Each node refers to a requirement or an artifact stored in the relational database.

Edge fields:
- id
- project_id
- source_node_type
- source_node_id
- target_node_type
- target_node_id
- relation_type
- propagation_enabled (boolean)
- relationship_strength (0.0 to 1.0)
- confirmation_status (CONFIRMED / SUGGESTED / NEEDS_REVIEW)
- notes
- created_by
- created_at
- updated_at
- is_active

Suggested relationship types:
- IMPLEMENTED_BY
- SPECIFIED_BY
- REALISED_AS
- DEPENDS_ON
- EXPOSES
- CONSUMED_BY
- USES
- VERIFIED_BY
- DOCUMENTED_BY
- DEPLOYED_IN
- CONSTRAINED_BY
- DERIVED_FROM

Every edge must have a clear direction meaning: "if the source changes, the target may need review." The UI should explain this convention when users create links.

Example graph:
REQ-003 Event Registration
  -> MOD-002 Registration Service       [IMPLEMENTED_BY]
  -> AC-003-01 OTP Verification Rule    [SPECIFIED_BY]
  -> TEST-012 Registration Test         [VERIFIED_BY]

MOD-002 Registration Service
  -> API-004 Register Event API         [EXPOSES]
  -> DB-006 Registration Record         [USES]

API-004 Register Event API
  -> UI-007 Event Registration Screen   [CONSUMED_BY]
  -> TEST-018 API Integration Test      [VERIFIED_BY]

DB-006 Registration Record
  -> API-004 Register Event API         [USED_BY]

The above is sample data. The actual graph must be created and editable by users.

Graph UI requirements:
- Pan and zoom.
- Display nodes with distinct type icons/colours.
- Show relationship labels.
- Click a node to open its detail panel.
- Click an edge to inspect relation type and notes.
- Filter by artifact type, owner, status or priority.
- Highlight the affected path for a selected change request.
- Provide a reset view and fit-to-screen action.
- Avoid rendering every node at once in large projects; support focused subgraph views around a selected requirement.

14. REQUIREMENT CHANGE DIFF ENGINE
===============================================================================

Input:
- Current approved requirement version.
- Proposed requirement fields from a change request.

Output:
A list of changes, each with:
- field_name
- old_value
- new_value
- diff_type: ADDED / REMOVED / MODIFIED / UNCHANGED
- category

Example:
Field: description
Old: Students can register for events.
New: Students can register for events after OTP verification.
Diff type: MODIFIED
Category: FUNCTIONAL_SCOPE_CHANGE

Field: acceptance_criteria
Old: User receives a registration confirmation.
New: User must verify a valid OTP before registration is confirmed.
Diff type: MODIFIED
Category: ACCEPTANCE_CRITERIA_CHANGE

Implementation note:
Start with field-level comparison and normalised text comparison. Do not overcomplicate with embeddings or semantic models. Optional AI assistance can be added later as a separate suggestion layer, and a human must confirm its output.

===============================================================================
15. IMPACT ANALYSIS ENGINE — CORE ALGORITHM
===============================================================================

15.1 Purpose
Given a changed requirement node, find downstream engineering artifacts that may need review and explain why they are included.

15.2 Inputs
- Project ID.
- Change request ID.
- Changed requirement node ID.
- Proposed requirement diff.
- Active traceability edges for that project.
- Artifact metadata (criticality, owner, status and verification coverage).

15.3 Outputs
For each impacted artifact:
- Artifact ID and title.
- Artifact type.
- Shortest dependency distance from the changed requirement.
- One or more explanation paths.
- Strongest selected path relationship strength.
- Criticality factor.
- Verification-gap factor.
- Final impact-priority score.
- Priority band.
- Analysis warnings, if any.

15.4 Traversal rules
1. Build an adjacency list using active edges where propagation_enabled = true.
2. Start at the changed requirement node.
3. Use breadth-first search (BFS) to explore downstream nodes.
4. Track visited nodes and shortest distance so cycles cannot cause infinite traversal.
5. Keep predecessor information so explanation paths can be reconstructed.
6. If multiple shortest paths exist, retain up to three representative paths for explanation.
7. Do not follow inactive or explicitly non-propagating edges.
8. Do not cross project boundaries.
9. Return a warning if the changed requirement has no outgoing links.
10. Mark suggested/unconfirmed links as weaker evidence and display that distinction in the report.

15.5 Pseudocode

function analyseImpact(startNode, graph):
    queue = [startNode]
    distance[startNode] = 0
    predecessor[startNode] = []
    visited = {startNode}

    while queue is not empty:
        current = queue.pop_front()
        for edge in graph.outgoing(current):
            if edge is inactive or propagation is disabled:
                continue
            nextNode = edge.target
            candidateDistance = distance[current] + 1

            if nextNode is not visited:
                visited.add(nextNode)
                distance[nextNode] = candidateDistance
                predecessor[nextNode] = [edge]
                queue.push_back(nextNode)
            else if candidateDistance == distance[nextNode]:
                add edge as an alternative shortest-path predecessor

    for every reached node except startNode:
        reconstruct up to three shortest explanation paths
        calculate path strength and score components
        create impact result

    return impact results and graph warnings

This pseudocode is a design guide. The implementation must handle typed node identifiers so that a requirement ID cannot be confused with an artifact ID.

15.6 Relationship strength
Store a relationship_strength between 0.0 and 1.0 on every edge. Use reasonable defaults by relation type, but allow authorised users to adjust them.

Example defaults (initial proposal, configurable):
- IMPLEMENTED_BY: 1.00
- REALISED_AS: 0.95
- VERIFIED_BY: 0.95
- DEPENDS_ON: 0.90
- EXPOSES: 0.85
- CONSUMED_BY: 0.85
- USES: 0.80
- SPECIFIED_BY: 0.90
- CONSTRAINED_BY: 0.90
- DOCUMENTED_BY: 0.60
- DERIVED_FROM: 0.90
- DEPLOYED_IN: 0.70

These values are heuristic defaults, not empirical probabilities.

For a path with edges e1 ... en:
path_strength = product(edge.relationship_strength for every edge in path)

If multiple shortest paths reach a node, use the strongest shortest-path strength for scoring and display the alternative paths separately.

15.7 Impact-priority scoring
Important: The score is a transparent REVIEW PRIORITY heuristic. It is not the probability that an artifact will fail, not a prediction of financial loss and not a guarantee of impact.

Define all factors between 0 and 1:
D = selected path strength.
P = proximity factor = 1 / shortest_path_distance.
C = artifact criticality / 5, where criticality is an integer from 1 to 5.
V = verification-gap factor:
    1.00 = no linked verification/test artifact found
    0.75 = linked test exists but is failing
    0.50 = test exists but is stale/not rerun after relevant changes
    0.10 = linked test has a current passing result

Initial score formula:
Impact Priority Score = round(100 * (0.30*D + 0.20*P + 0.25*C + 0.25*V))

Priority bands:
75–100 = CRITICAL
55–74  = HIGH
35–54  = MEDIUM
0–34   = LOW

Project settings may allow a project owner to tune weights, but the weights must total 1.0 and changes must be recorded in audit history. Keep a default configuration for repeatable demonstrations.

Example:
A database artifact has path strength 0.90, distance 2, criticality 5/5, and no linked current test.
D = 0.90
P = 0.50
C = 1.00
V = 1.00
Score = round(100 * (0.30*0.90 + 0.20*0.50 + 0.25*1.00 + 0.25*1.00))
Score = round(86) = 86, CRITICAL

The report should show the component values and formula so that the result is understandable. Avoid presenting the score as scientific certainty.

15.8 Analysis warnings
- Requirement has no outgoing traceability links.
- One or more related edges are unconfirmed.
- Important artifact has no owner.
- Critical artifact has no linked test.
- Artifact has no recent review date.
- Graph contains cycles (traversal safely handled them).
- Some artifacts are archived or have inactive relationships.
- Analysis uses a baseline or graph snapshot that has changed since the previous run.

15.9 Reproducibility
Every saved analysis must record:
- analysis ID and timestamp
- change request ID
- source requirement version ID
- proposed change snapshot or diff hash
- traceability graph snapshot/version marker
- scoring weights and algorithm version
- generated result set
This allows a previous report to remain understandable after the project graph changes.

===============================================================================
17. CHANGE DEBT AND TRACEABILITY HEALTH
===============================================================================

17.1 Change debt
Change debt is unresolved work associated with approved changes.

Track:
- Impact results not yet reviewed.
- Required artifact updates not completed.
- Required retests not executed.
- Failed or blocked tests.
- Overdue remediation actions.
- Accepted risks that require follow-up.

Dashboard cards:
- Open approved changes.
- Unreviewed high/critical impacts.
- Overdue remediation actions.
- Pending retests.
- Failed tests after changes.
- Accepted risks.

Optional weighted change-debt indicator:
For each unresolved impact, use criticality weight (1–5) multiplied by an age factor. Sum the values and label the result as an internal project indicator, not a standard industry metric. A simpler count-based dashboard is acceptable for MVP.

17.2 Traceability health
Indicators:
- Requirements with at least one implementation relationship.
- Requirements with at least one verification relationship.
- Artifacts with no incoming or outgoing relationships.
- Critical artifacts without linked tests.
- Unconfirmed relationships.
- Archived artifacts still connected through active edges.

Display numerator and denominator where possible. Example: "18 of 22 requirements have a verification link" is more transparent than an unexplained score of 82%.

17.3 Requirement volatility
For each requirement, calculate the number of approved changes within a selected period. Show a simple trend and label repeatedly changed requirements as "high volatility" based on a configurable threshold. Do not claim that a high count automatically means poor quality; it is a signal for review.

18. CHANGE REQUEST STATE MACHINE
===============================================================================

Suggested statuses:
DRAFT
SUBMITTED
ANALYSIS_READY
IN_REVIEW
APPROVED
REJECTED
DEFERRED
IMPLEMENTING
VERIFICATION_PENDING
CLOSED
CANCELLED

Allowed flow:
DRAFT -> SUBMITTED
SUBMITTED -> ANALYSIS_READY
ANALYSIS_READY -> IN_REVIEW
IN_REVIEW -> APPROVED / REJECTED / DEFERRED
DEFERRED -> SUBMITTED or CANCELLED
APPROVED -> IMPLEMENTING
IMPLEMENTING -> VERIFICATION_PENDING
VERIFICATION_PENDING -> IMPLEMENTING (if tests fail or work remains)
VERIFICATION_PENDING -> CLOSED (when closure rules pass)
DRAFT -> CANCELLED
SUBMITTED -> CANCELLED (with permission)

Enforce transitions in the backend. The frontend must not be the source of workflow truth.

Approval conditions:
- An impact analysis exists for the current proposal and current graph snapshot.
- The approver has permission.
- The decision has a timestamp and actor.
- Rejection/defer decisions include rationale.
- Approval creates the next immutable requirement version.
- Approval records the analysis snapshot used for the decision.

Closure conditions (initial policy):
- All impacted artifacts have a recorded disposition.
- All UPDATE_REQUIRED actions are completed or explicitly waived by an authorised reviewer.
- All mandatory RETEST_REQUIRED actions have a passing test result or a documented risk acceptance.
- No critical/high item remains silently unreviewed.
- Any residual risk has a reason and responsible approver.

===============================================================================
24. TESTING AND QUALITY PLAN
===============================================================================

24.1 Unit tests — graph engine
- Linear path A -> B -> C.
- Branching graph A -> B and A -> C.
- Multiple shortest paths to one node.
- Graph containing a cycle.
- Disconnected artifact.
- Inactive edge ignored.
- Propagation-disabled edge ignored.
- Cross-project edge rejected.
- No outgoing links warning.
- Typed IDs with identical display strings do not collide.

24.2 Unit tests — scoring
- Score remains between 0 and 100.
- High criticality increases priority when other factors are equal.
- Missing test coverage increases priority when other factors are equal.
- Greater path distance reduces proximity factor.
- Relationship strength affects path score.
- Priority bands use correct boundaries.
- Configured weights must total 1.0.

24.3 Unit tests — diff and workflow
- Added/removed/modified fields are correctly classified.
- An approved requirement version cannot be overwritten.
- Invalid workflow transitions are rejected.
- Rejection/defer requires rationale.
- Approval creates a new version transactionally.
- Simulation does not modify approved data.
- Closure is blocked while mandatory actions remain incomplete.

24.4 Integration tests
- User creates a project and requirements.
- User creates artifacts and edges.
- User submits a change request.
- Backend generates a diff and impact analysis.
- Approver approves the change.
- New version and remediation actions are created.
- Tester records a result.
- Change request closes only when rules pass.
- User from another project cannot access the data.

24.5 End-to-end browser tests
Use Playwright to test the main user journey from login through change approval and closure. Test important empty/error/loading states too.

24.6 Manual acceptance checks
- The app runs from a clean clone using documented instructions.
- Demo data can be seeded reliably.
- No page relies on hardcoded fake metrics.
- Impact analysis results are calculated from stored graph edges.

30. DESIGN DECISIONS AND IMPORTANT WARNINGS
===============================================================================

30.1 Do not overpromise automatic discovery
The first version analyses dependencies that the team has recorded. It cannot reliably infer all dependencies from a requirement's wording alone. Make traceability completeness a first-class dashboard concept.

30.2 Do not make AI the core dependency
An LLM may suggest possible links or summarise a change later. Such suggestions must be marked as unconfirmed until a user accepts them. Deterministic graph traversal remains the source of impact results.

30.3 Do not create an overcomplicated score
A transparent weighted formula is easier to implement, test and explain. Display the formula and its inputs. Call it a review-priority score.

30.4 Do not build too many modules before proving the central workflow
The essential vertical slice is:
Create requirement -> create artifacts -> link graph -> submit change -> analyse impact -> review -> approve -> remediate -> retest -> close.
Make that workflow reliable before adding optional features.

30.5 Do not use microservices
A modular monolith is appropriate for a student team and a single deployable application.

30.6 Do not use fake data in the live interface
Seed data is useful for demonstration, but every count, chart, graph and report must be generated from the database. Clearly identify demo projects as sample data.

30.7 Preserve historical meaning
When the graph changes, old impact reports should remain tied to the graph snapshot and requirement version used at the time of analysis.

32. FINAL PRODUCT DEFINITION
===============================================================================

ReqRipple is a web-based, traceability-driven software requirements change-management platform. It helps a software team understand the ripple effects of changing a requirement by comparing versions, traversing a directed dependency graph, identifying affected engineering artifacts, explaining impact paths, assigning transparent review priorities, supporting human approval and tracking remediation and verification until closure.

Its strongest differentiators are:
1. Explainable direct and indirect impact paths.
2. A what-if change simulation that does not alter approved baselines.
3. A change-debt dashboard for unresolved impact work.
4. Traceability health and missing-test detection.
5. Immutable requirement version history and auditable decisions.
6. A deterministic, testable impact engine that works without paid AI APIs.

Build the project around the end-to-end change lifecycle. A smaller, reliable product with real graph analysis and excellent usability will be stronger than a large application filled with incomplete features.
